# Who to reach and how (plan only; nothing has been posted)

## Audience
1. Digital nomads and remote workers moving through Europe (most searches, most tools, price-tolerant).
2. Long-stay visitors from visa-exempt countries (Korea, Japan, US, Latin America) who plan multi-month Europe trips.
3. People tracking a 183-day tax limit (expats, cross-border workers).
Korean, Japanese, Chinese and Spanish pages exist so each can be reached in its own language.

## Channels, in order of effort
1. **Search (slow, free).** Per-language landing pages with hreflang, FAQ markup and sitemap are in place. Target queries to check in Search Console after a few weeks: "schengen 90/180 calculator", "솅겐 90일 계산기", "シェンゲン 90日 計算", "申根 90天 计算器", "calculadora schengen 90/180". Search volume and difficulty were **not measured**.
2. **Community posts (days).** Digital-nomad and expat forums, Korean travel and "유럽 장기 체류" communities, Japanese and Spanish nomad groups. Read each community's rules; many ban self-promotion. Prefer answering existing "how do I count" questions with the tool as one option, and disclose that you built it.
3. **Product Hunt / Show HN style launch (one day).** Use the drafts in `copy-and-posts.md`. Pick a weekday, be around to answer comments.
4. **Directories and lists.** Submit to "tools for digital nomads" lists and alternatives pages for the existing Schengen calculator apps.

## Measure
No analytics are installed. Cheapest signals: Search Console impressions, Gumroad sales, support email, and comments on the posts. Decide a bar up front (e.g. N sales in 30 days) for whether to keep investing.

## Risks
- The category already has many apps (checked app-store listings by title only). Expect to compete on privacy, planning, and one-time price, not on being first.
- A wrong or misunderstood count can cost users real money, so keep the "estimate only" wording prominent and answer corrections quickly.
